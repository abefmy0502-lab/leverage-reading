# Landing pages of top-grossing global habit / learning / wellness / productivity subscription apps + conversion research

Research date: 2026-10-11. **Method caveat (important for the writer):** live pages could not be fetched from this environment (the agent proxy refused direct connections to duolingo.com, headspace.com, calm.com, strava.com, notion.com, etc., and also web.archive.org). Every quote below comes from search-engine snippets of the official page, its cached or localized copies, Wayback captures, App Store listings, or third-party teardowns. Labels used:
- **[V-official]** = exact wording seen in a snippet of an official page or a mirrored copy of it (wording reliable, date of the live page not confirmed)
- **[V-secondary]** = exact wording quoted by a third party (teardown, review, archive), with a date where known
- **[P]** = paraphrase or summary; not exact wording

## 1. Hero headlines and sub-headlines: exact wording and pattern (outcome / identity / social)

### Takeaway
The category leaders use **very short, 3–8 word heroes** built on one of three patterns. **Outcome + adjectives**: Duolingo, "The free, fun, and effective way to learn a language". **Feeling or identity**: Headspace, "Be kind to your mind"; Day One, "Your journal for life". **Social or community**: Strava, "Community-Powered Motivation". The sub-headline almost always carries a **category-leadership claim** ("#1 …") or a **scale number** ("over 100 million").

### Cited Findings
- **Duolingo**: hero "The free, fun, and effective way to learn a language!" [V-secondary, screenshot of duolingo.com dated 2024-12-24] — [TypingMind landing-page audit](https://custom.typingmind.com/tools/landing-page-audit/www.duolingo.com). The same line is used as Duolingo's Product Hunt tagline — [Product Hunt](https://www.producthunt.com/products/duolingo/launches/duolingo-max).
- **Duolingo**: the 2021 homepage used "The world's #1 way to learn a language" [V-secondary, Wayback 2021]. This means Duolingo moved from a leadership claim to a **"free" + benefit-adjective** claim — [Wayback capture](https://webcf.waybackmachine.org/web/20211019143910/https://zs.duolingo.com).
- **Headspace**: headline "Be kind to your mind" with a sub-head about reducing stress and feeling happier [V-official headline; sub-head P]. Other pages use the same headline with the tagline "A healthier, happier you starts right here" [V-official]. Headspace runs many campaign variants of the same headline (/newyear "Jumpstart your year", /subscribe 50%-off, /sem-unemployed) — [Headspace subscription](https://www.headspace.com/headspace-subscription); [Headspace /buy](https://www.headspace.com/buy); [Headspace /newyear](https://www.headspace.com/newyear); [Headspace /subscribe](https://www.headspace.com/subscribe).

Another snapshot says "the AI workspace where teams and AI agents get more done together" [V-official fragments; exact hero order not confirmed] — [notion.com](https://notion.com); [Notion help](https://www.notion.com/help/category/new-to-notion/all).

### Inferences
- Pattern counts across the confirmed heroes:
  - **Leadership claim "#1"**: Calm, Todoist, Day One (sub), Duolingo 2021.
  - **Benefit adjectives + "free"**: Duolingo 2024.
  - **Emotional / identity**: Headspace, Day One ("for life").
  - **Social**: Strava.
  - **Category definition**: Notion "AI workspace".
- The "#1" claims at Calm are **footnoted to a dated third-party source** (Sensor Tower Q2 2024). For a new app without rank data, this pattern is not available, and advertising rules make such claims risky without a source.
- Duolingo's shift from "#1" to "free, fun, effective" suggests that once scale is self-evident, leaders spend the hero on **objection-handling** ("free") plus **experience** ("fun"), not rank.
- "Finally" (Todoist) is a pain-acknowledgement device: it implies the reader has tried and failed before.

### Gaps
- Current (2026) live hero text for calm.com, headspace.com root, duolingo.com, thefabulous.co, noom.com, bear.app and dayoneapp.com could not be verified (fetch blocked). The dates above are 2024–2025 captures.
- I could not confirm whether Todoist ever used "Clarity, finally".

## 2. CTA wording and mobile vs desktop handling

### Takeaway
CTAs split into **"Join"** (Strava: "Join Us Now"), **"Get … free"** (Notion: "Get Notion free"), and **"Try for free"** (Headspace). The free-ness is usually **inside the button** or immediately next to it. Strava pairs a primary CTA with an exploratory secondary ("Explore Strava"). I found no reliable primary-source evidence on each brand's mobile-vs-desktop CTA switch; app-install mechanics are covered in Q6.

### Cited Findings
- **Strava** buttons: "Join Us Now" and "Explore Strava", plus "Join … for free" in the body [V-official] — [Strava](https://www.strava.com/?hl=en-eu).
- **Headspace**: a "Try for free" button under "Be kind to your mind" [P, from snippet summary] — [Headspace subscription](https://www.headspace.com/headspace-subscription).
- **Notion**: the "Get Notion free" button sits in site navigation and on themed landing pages [V-official] — [Notion /severance](https://www.notion.com/severance); [Notion help](https://www.notion.com/help/category/new-to-notion/all).
- **Noom**: the entry points reported are "SEE IF YOU QUALIFY" or "START YOUR TRIAL", leading to a long quiz. The pricing page shows a 15-minute countdown [P; source is an unverifiable internal-looking dashboard, low reliability] — [funnel screenshots](https://funnel-researcher-dashboard.internal.trytwofold.com/screenshots/noom).
- **Duolingo**: the "Get started" and "I already have an account" button pair could not be confirmed from any source in this session (see Gaps).

### Inferences
- Leaders put "free" into the CTA itself ("Get Notion free", "Try for free", "Join … for free"). This removes the price objection at the click point.
- Noom's "See if you qualify" is a **commitment / exclusivity** CTA that starts a long quiz funnel. The pattern is high-converting for weight loss but uses urgency (countdown timers) that may conflict with trust-first brands.

### Gaps
- Primary evidence on how each brand changes CTAs by device was not obtainable (for example, Duolingo or Headspace showing an App Store badge on mobile vs "Get started" on desktop).
- No A/B test data on "Get started" vs "Try free" vs "Download" wording was found in reputable public sources.

## 3. Social proof (numbers, App Store rating, awards, Editor's Choice)

### Takeaway
Leaders use **one big scale number** ("100 million+"), **"#1" with a cited source**, and **Apple awards** (App of the Year, Best of 2018). The research evidence favors **specific, verifiable, named** proof over generic praise. However, most NN/g-attributed statistics circulating online could not be traced to nngroup.com.

### Cited Findings
- **Strava**: "Join over 100 million active people on Strava" [V-official] — [Strava](https://www.strava.com/?hl=en-eu).
- **Calm**: "#1 app" with a Sensor Tower Q2 2024 footnote [V-official] — [Calm About](https://www.calm.com/about). Its App Store listing title shows "** Best of 2018 Award Winner ** — Apple" [V-official] — [App Store UA](https://apps.apple.com/ua/app/id571800810). An older page says "over 100 million downloads" — [Calm Shop](https://shop.calm.com/pages/about).
- **Day One**: "Apple's App of the Year with over 15 million downloads and 200,000 5-star ratings globally" [V-official, App Store listing] — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id1055511498).
- **Todoist**: claims "world's #1 task manager and to-do list app" [P/near-verbatim] — [todoist.com](https://todoist.com/).
- **Fabulous**: its credibility comes from the institution (Duke Center for Advanced Hindsight, Dan Ariely as advisor) [P] — [Idealist](https://www.idealist.org/en/business/624dea068a5a455db19876157218f513-the-fabulous-paris).
- **Research on proof** (secondary; NN/g originals not located):
  - Named, photo-attached quotes are said to beat many anonymous ones. Perceived trust is said to rise about 20% in "NN/g's 2023 testimonial roundup" [P, unverified attribution] — [jwatte blog](https://jwatte.com/blog/blog-social-proof-eeat-signals/).
  - Users are said to spend about 57% of viewing time above the fold [P, attributed to NN/g, unverified] — [AboveFoldLab](https://t.me/s/AboveFoldLab/8).
  - First-name-only testimonials, stock photos and uniformly five-star ratings can read as manufactured; some visible criticism can raise believability [P] — [Switas glossary](https://www.switas.com/glossary/social-proof).
  - Practitioner advice is to place proof next to decision points (CTA, pricing) [P] — [DesignProject](https://designproject.io/blog/incorporating-social-proof-testimonials-case-studies-and-logos/).

### Inferences
- A pre-launch or new app has no scale number or rank. Credible substitutes seen in this category:
  - **Institutional or method credibility** (Fabulous / Duke).
  - **A named founder or author voice.**
  - **Real, named early-user quotes.**
  - Later, the App Store rating once it exists.
- Unsourced "#1" claims should be avoided; Calm's footnoting shows the leaders' standard.

### Gaps
- No primary NN/g article on testimonials or ratings was retrievable in this session. Treat the % figures above as unverified.
- No quantitative data was found on the conversion lift from showing App Store star ratings or "Editor's Choice" on a web LP.

## 4. Price display: anchoring, per-day/per-month framing, annual emphasis, free tier vs trial

### Takeaway
Wellness and learning leaders show the **annual plan as the default**. They express it **as a monthly equivalent** (Calm $69.99/yr ≈ $5.83/mo; Duolingo Super $59.99/yr = "$5/month … billed annually") and attach a **longer trial to annual** (Headspace: annual 14 days free vs monthly 7 days). Freemium apps (Duolingo, Notion, Day One, Bear, Strava) lead with "free" and keep price off the hero. The academic support for small-unit framing is Gourville's "pennies-a-day" (JCR 1998). RevenueCat data shows annual-plan trial conversion rising with trial length.

### Cited Findings
- **Headspace**: annual plan "14 days free" vs monthly "7 days free". "After your free trial, the annual subscription is $69.99 USD and automatically renews each year" [V-official, cached]. A promo variant reads "50% off your first year, then subscription will renew at full price" [V-official] — [Headspace subscription](https://www.headspace.com/headspace-subscription); [Headspace subscribe](https://www.headspace.com/subscribe).
- **Calm**: $69.99/year (about $5.83/month); $14.99/month; 7-day free trial; lifetime $399.99 or $499.99 (sources conflict) [P, aggregators 2025–2026] — [ThePricer](https://www.thepricer.org/how-much-does-calm-app-cost/); [FrontDeskReview (June 2026)](https://frontdeskreview.com/software/meditation-apps/calm/).
- **Duolingo Super**: $12.99/month; annual reported as $59.99 ("$5/monthly, $59.99/year billed annually") or $83.99 (≈$7/mo) depending on source and date. Family plan about $119.99/yr for up to 6 users [P, third-party, conflicting] — [ToolRadar](https://toolradar.com/tools/duolingo/pricing); [ThePricer](https://www.thepricer.org/how-much-does-super-duolingo-cost/).
- **Bear Pro**: $2.99/month or $29.99/year (≈15% saving). The free tier covers local notes, scanning, export and 3 themes; trial length is reported as 7 or 14 days (conflicting) [P] — [ToolRadar Bear](https://toolradar.com/tools/bear/pricing). In 2023 Bear raised prices for new customers only and kept existing subscribers at $1.49/mo or $14.99/yr [V-official] — [Bear blog 2023](https://blog.bear.app/2023/04/bear-2-will-have-a-new-price-only-for-new-customers/).
- **Day One**: "free with a Premium upgrade" is stated on the homepage next to the hero [P, Wayback 2024] — [Wayback](https://webcf.waybackmachine.org/web/20240704145136/https://dayoneapp.com).
- **Noom**: $70 monthly and $209 annual (≈$17.41/mo), as of December 2023. Price is revealed only after the quiz [P] — [Medical News Today](https://www.medicalnewstoday.com/articles/327114).
- **Fabulous**: "starting at $39.99/yr" vs "$8–15 per month" (conflicting third-party sources) [P] — [Nibble review](https://nibble-app.com/blog/fabulous-app-review).
- **Per-day framing research**: Gourville, "Pennies-a-Day: The Effect of Temporal Reframing on Transaction Evaluation", *Journal of Consumer Research* 24(4):395–408, 1998.
  - Restating a lump sum as small daily amounts makes consumers compare it with small ongoing expenses. Lab studies supported its effectiveness across categories — [RePEc abstract](https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/jconrs/v24y1998i4p395-408.html).
  - An "85 cents per day" vs "$300 per year" donation example (52% vs 30%) is a secondhand claim, unverified — [Psychology Today](https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/blog/the-science-behind-behavior/201904/the-powerful-influence-pennies-day-price-offers).
- **Anchoring / decoy**: the Economist decoy example is reported as 68% choosing Digital ($59) of two options. Adding a Print-only $125 decoy moved Print+Digital from 32% to 84% (Ariely, as relayed by a blog). Showing a fake "was" price erodes trust [P] — [AboveFoldLab](https://t.me/s/AboveFoldLab?before=49); [Xictron](https://www.xictron.com/en/blog/pricing-psychology-price-display-online-shop-2026/).
- **Trial length (RevenueCat data)**: on annual subscriptions, trial-to-paid conversion is reported as:

  | Trial length | Trial-to-paid conversion |
  |---|---|
  | ≤4 days | 24% |
  | 5–9 days | 33% |
  | 10–16 days | 43% |
  | 17–32 days | 44.6% |

  RevenueCat's 2026 report cites a 42.5% median for 17+ day trials vs 25.5% for ≤4 days [P, via SaaStr and the RevenueCat blog] — [SaaStr](https://saastr.com/what-17000-subscription-apps-tell-us-about-free-trial-length-annual-plans-convert-86-better-with-30-day-trials-monthly-tops-out-at-two-weeks-and-ai-apps-hit-a-wall-at-16-days); [RevenueCat blog: free trial length](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/free-trial-length).
- **RevenueCat State of Subscription Apps 2025** (75,000+ apps):
  - 30% of annual subscribers cancel within the first month.
  - The top 5% of apps earn 400× the bottom 25%.
  - AI apps earn about $0.63 revenue per install at 60 days vs a $0.31 median.

  [P, secondary] — [Subscription Insider](https://subscriptioninsider.com/article-type/news/revenuecats-state-of-subscription-apps-2025-report-ais-dominance-retention-challenges-and-the-shift-away-from-pure-subscriptions); [RevenueCat report page](https://revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2025).
- **Adapty** (vendor data):
  - The best 1-year-LTV setup is a weekly plan with a 3-day trial; the worst is annual with no trial.
  - Health & Fitness is the only category where the annual share keeps growing.
  - Price tests win on conversion only 28.3% of the time; judge on revenue, not conversion.
  - Raising weekly prices from $7.99 to $9.99 cut conversion 5–8% but raised ARPU 18–22%.

  [P] — [Adapty high-performing paywall 2026](https://adapty.io/blog/markdown/high-performing-paywall-2026.md); [Adapty experiments playbook](https://adapty.io/blog/markdown/paywall-experiments-playbook.md).
- **Adapty Prosto case**: adding bi-annual and monthly options next to a $30 annual plan and changing the art produced a "30% revenue increase" [P, vendor] — [Adapty Prosto](https://adapty.io/case-studies/prosto/).

### Inferences
- The standard pattern for wellness and learning: **annual = default and highlighted, priced as "≈ $X/month", longer trial on annual, monthly shown as the anchor.** Calm's $14.99 monthly makes $5.83/mo look like a large saving.
- Per-day framing has academic support (Gourville). Among these leaders, per-month equivalents appear more often than per-day ones on web pages. Per-day framing appears mostly inside in-app paywalls and quiz funnels such as Noom (unverified for 2025–26).
- Freemium apps keep price **off the hero**: Day One states "free with Premium upgrade", Strava says "for free", Notion's button says "Get Notion free". Price lives on a separate pricing page.
- Trial length matters: the RevenueCat data suggests a 7-day trial on annual plans sits in the 33% band and a 14-day trial in the 43% band. Headspace's 14-day annual / 7-day monthly split matches this.

### Gaps
- I could not see the current on-page price layout (e.g., "Most popular" badges, strike-through monthly totals) for Calm, Headspace or Duolingo because live fetch was blocked.
- No reputable controlled test of per-day vs per-month framing on a subscription-app web LP was found.
- The RevenueCat 2025 PDF itself (gated) was not read. Its exact trial and annual figures are relayed by secondary sources.

## 5. Community and sharing (Strava)

### Takeaway
Strava is the one leader whose **hero is about community, not the individual outcome**. The copy explicitly sequences tracking → community ("Join for the tracking, stay for the community"). Community is presented as the reason to stay (retention), while tracking is the reason to join (acquisition).

### Cited Findings
- "Community-Powered Motivation" / "Track your progress and cheer each other on." / "Join for the tracking, stay for the community." [V-official] — [Strava](https://www.strava.com/?v3=true).
- The page calls Strava a social network for athletes that works with a phone or GPS device [P] — [Strava](https://www.strava.com/?hl=en-eu).
- A teardown critiques the headline as abstract; it would prefer concrete outcomes [P, opinion] — [Landing Doctor](https://landingdoctors.com/es/teardowns/strava-com).

### Inferences
- The "join for X, stay for Y" structure fits any app whose long-term value differs from its first-session value. For a reading-memo app, the analogue is "join to keep notes, stay for the advisor your notes become".
- Strava's community proof (100M+) is also a scale claim, so community and social proof reinforce each other.

### Gaps
- I could not retrieve details of how Strava's web LP shows shareable activity images or kudos visually in 2025–26.

## 6. Research evidence on what raises LP conversion for mobile subscription apps (headline, CTA, proof, price, length, install mechanics)

### Takeaway
The firmest public data:
- **Unbounce 2024**: median LP conversion is 6.6%. Pages written at a 5th–7th grade reading level convert at 11.1% vs 5.3% for college-level copy, roughly a 2× gap.
- **RevenueCat**: longer trials raise annual-plan conversion.
- **Gourville**: per-day framing works.

Evidence on Smart App Banners and QR codes is **vendor-only and anecdotal**. No independent, controlled studies were found.

### Cited Findings
- **Unbounce 2024** (41,000 LPs, 464M pageviews, 57M conversions):
  - Median conversion rate is 6.6% across industries; "good" means the 75th percentile [P] — [Unbounce](https://unbounce.com/?p=146790); [Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/how-does-your-landing-page-performance-compare-453224).
  - Copy at a 5th–7th grade reading level converts at an 11.1% median vs 5.3% at college level. SaaS pages are the hardest to read; fitness and nutrition marketers are the "wordiest" [P] — [MarketingProfs summary](https://marketingprofs.com/charts/2024/52374/landing-page-conversion-benchmarks).
- **Unbounce 2020**: median 3.2%; top-quartile median 25% (a different dataset, not comparable) — [Unbounce 2020](https://unbounce.com/conversion-benchmark-report-2020/).
- **Page length**: no numeric word-count vs conversion relationship was found in accessible summaries — [MarketingProfs](https://marketingprofs.com/charts/2024/52374/landing-page-conversion-benchmarks).
- **Smart App Banner**:
  - Apple's banner is a single meta tag with Apple-controlled design. It cannot be customized for conversion and handles deep links poorly; custom banners allow brand copy and deep links [P] — [Branch](https://www.branch.io/resources/blog/how-to-use-smart-banners-to-increase-app-downloads/).
  - Vendor claims: "double digit" lift in mobile web-to-app conversion [P, vendor] — [U2L guide 2026](https://u2l.ai/blog/smart-app-banner-guide); one retailer saw "a 300% increase in app downloads in the first two weeks" [P, vendor anecdote] — [URLgenius](https://app.urlgeni.us/blog/urlgenius-smart-banners).
  - Best practice: give a concrete reason rather than "download our app", hide the banner for users already in the app or who dismissed it, and remember the dismissal [P] — [U2L](https://u2l.ai/blog/smart-app-banner-guide); [MobiLoud](https://www.mobiloud.com/help/knowledge-base/smart-banners).
- **QR for desktop**: desktop visitors cannot open an app link directly, so a QR state is a separate flow. No test data was found [P] — [Detour docs](https://detour.swmansion.com/docs/Fundamentals/smart-banners); [Airbridge whitepaper](https://www.airbridge.io/library/web-to-app-conversion-whitepaper).
- **Price tests** rarely raise conversion (28.3% win rate) but often raise revenue. Measure revenue per visitor rather than click-through [P, vendor] — [Adapty](https://adapty.io/blog/markdown/high-performing-paywall-2026.md).

### Inferences
- The strongest actionable, evidence-backed lever for copy is **plain language**: short, concrete words. This matches the leaders' 3–8 word heroes.
- For install pages, the best-supported setup is:
  - Mobile Safari: Apple Smart App Banner plus an in-page App Store badge.
  - Desktop: a QR code.
  - Separate tracking per channel (e.g., App Store Connect campaign links) because public evidence is weak.
- Because annual subscribers cancel early (30% in the first month, per RevenueCat), the LP should set accurate expectations. Over-promising heroes may raise installs but hurt retention.

### Gaps
- No CXL-published study was retrieved on per-day framing, decoys, or CTA wording for app LPs; several "CXL says X%" stats online could not be traced.
- No primary NN/g research on mobile app install pages was retrieved.
- No independent A/B data comparing the Apple Smart App Banner, custom banners and QR codes was found; all figures are vendor marketing.
- The Unbounce full report (page-length analysis, CTA-wording effects) was not accessible beyond summaries.
